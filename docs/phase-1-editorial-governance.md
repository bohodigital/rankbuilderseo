# Part I: Editorial mission

## 2. The central strategy

Rank Builder will become the best independent troubleshooting and verification resource for people trying to understand why search traffic, crawling, indexing, canonicalization, analytics, or an SEO provider is not behaving as expected.

The Phase 1 position is:

> **Rank Builder explains what search systems are doing, shows how to verify it, and gives the reader a defensible next action.**

Rank Builder is not primarily:

- An SEO news blog
- A generic digital-marketing publication
- A beginner dictionary
- An agency sales funnel
- A keyword-volume content farm
- A collection of rewritten Google documentation
- A home for speculative algorithm folklore
- A venue for mass-produced platform permutations

The first topical territory is deliberately narrow:

> **Google indexing, crawling, canonicalization, Search Console diagnostics, and closely related implementation failures.**

This territory is large enough to support dozens of distinct high-intent pages, multiple tools, original research, and platform-specific implementation guides. It is also narrow enough to establish recognizable topical authority rather than scattering a young domain across every subject containing the word “SEO.”

## 3. Phase 1 traffic thesis

Rank Builder will not maximize traffic merely by maximizing page count.

The site must increase five separate factors:

`Organic Traffic = sum over queries of Demand × Visibility × CTR.`

For each relevant query:

- **Demand** is the number of searches or related user needs.
- **Visibility** is Rank Builder’s share of available impressions.
- **CTR** is the portion of impressions converted into visits.

Page volume increases the number of queries Rank Builder can address. It does not by itself produce rankings, links, trust, or clicks.

Phase 1 must therefore build four mutually reinforcing assets:

1. **Search coverage:** A page for each materially distinct diagnostic intent.
2. **Topical authority:** Dense, coherent coverage of one connected problem space.
3. **Noncommodity value:** Tools, diagrams, examples, data, and tested procedures that cannot be replaced by a summary.
4. **Linkable authority:** Original research and utilities that other websites have a reason to cite.

# Part II: Phase 1 scope

## 5. Phase 1 objective

Phase 1 will create a complete first-generation Google indexing and Search Console troubleshooting system.

The finished phase should contain:

- One definitive indexing-diagnostics hub
- Complete guides for major Page Indexing statuses
- Supporting crawling, sitemap, canonical, rendering, and recrawl guides
- A small set of genuinely distinct platform implementation guides
- Two functional public diagnostic tools
- One controlled indexing research program
- One initial research report
- A coherent internal-link graph connecting every asset
- Meaningful upgrades to the discovery and related-content metadata of existing publications

The target is approximately:

- **31 new editorial pages**
- **Two tool landing pages with working tools**
- **Two research pages**
- **Thirty-five total new public assets**
- **At least thirty-eight pages in the completed indexing cluster when the three existing anchor articles are included**

This is a management target, not permission to publish unfinished work merely to satisfy arithmetic.

## 6. Phase 1 completion conditions

Phase 1 is complete only when:

1. Every planned core indexing status has a materially distinct page.
2. Every published page has at least one meaningful inbound contextual link.
3. Every published page links to a parent, prerequisite, alternative diagnosis, or next action where relevant.
4. The cluster has one clear flagship entry point.
5. The two planned tools work without deceptive steps or mandatory registration.
6. The indexing study publishes a clear methodology and initial result.
7. All pages pass the repository’s canonical content and release gates.
8. Search Console provides page- and query-level performance data for the cluster.
9. The first update, merge, and pruning review has been completed.
10. No unresolved cannibalization or duplicate-intent defect remains.

Phase 1 completion is based on coverage and quality, not on reaching a promised traffic number.

# Part III: Audience and intent

## 8. Primary audiences

### 8.1 Site owners and publishers

They know a page or website is missing from Google but may not understand the distinction between discovery, crawling, indexing, canonicalization, and ranking.

They need:

- A plain explanation
- A way to confirm the issue
- A sequence of checks
- A warning against random changes
- A clear escalation path

### 8.2 Technical marketers and SEO practitioners

They understand Search Console terminology but need stronger diagnostic structure, implementation details, or evidence.

They need:

- Exact status interpretation
- Likely causes in order
- Platform implications
- Technical verification methods
- Reliable source references
- Limitations and edge cases

### 8.3 Developers

They need to determine whether a search problem originates in application behavior, headers, redirects, rendering, deployment, routing, canonical generation, or infrastructure.

They need:

- HTTP examples
- Rendered-versus-source distinctions
- Header inspection
- Framework-specific failure modes
- Reproducible tests
- Clear ownership boundaries

### 8.4 SEO buyers and managers

They need to understand whether a claimed technical problem is real, material, and being addressed competently.

They need:

- Evidence they can request
- Questions to ask a provider
- Signs of cargo-cult remediation
- Meaningful completion criteria
- A distinction between eligibility and guaranteed outcomes

## 9. Searcher-state model

Every page must identify the reader’s current state.

The core states are:

1. **Confused:** The reader does not understand a term or report.
2. **Diagnosing:** The reader knows the symptom but not the cause.
3. **Confirming:** The reader suspects a cause and needs evidence.
4. **Repairing:** The reader needs exact corrective steps.
5. **Waiting:** The repair is complete and the reader needs realistic monitoring guidance.
6. **Escalating:** The reader needs a developer, host, provider, or Google support path.
7. **Evaluating:** The reader is judging whether an SEO provider’s explanation is credible.

An article trying to serve every state equally often becomes swollen and unfocused. Each page must name its primary state and support no more than two adjacent states.

# Part IV: Topic selection

## 10. Candidate-topic sources

Topic discovery must draw from multiple evidence streams.

Priority sources are:

- Search Console queries and page impressions
- Google Page Indexing and URL Inspection terminology
- Google documentation headings and error labels
- Google autocomplete and related searches
- People Also Ask questions
- Bing autocomplete and related searches
- Google Trends
- Relevant Reddit and technical-forum questions
- Search Central Community discussions
- WordPress, Shopify, Next.js, Cloudflare, Wix, and other official support documentation
- GitHub issues for relevant frameworks
- Support tickets, audit findings, or recurring real-world problems
- Competitor coverage gaps
- Questions that existing Rank Builder articles naturally raise

No topic may be approved solely because a keyword database assigns it a large number.

Estimated volume can help order opportunities. It cannot establish that Rank Builder can or should publish the page.

## 11. Topic scoring model

Each candidate topic receives this score:

`S = clamp(4F + 4O + 3U + 3W + 2G + 2B + M + C - 3K - 2H, 0, 100)`

Each variable is scored from zero to five.

| Variable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| F | Fit with the active Phase 1 topical territory |
| O | Ability to add original or noncommodity value |
| U | Urgency and consequence of the searcher’s problem |
| W | Weakness, incompleteness, or confusion in existing results |
| G | Contribution to the internal-link and diagnostic graph |
| B | Backlink, citation, or distribution potential |
| M | Strategic or commercial relevance |
| C | Confidence that the intent and answer are understood |
| K | Cannibalization or duplicate-intent risk |
| H | Maintenance burden and factual volatility |

### Scoring interpretation

- **85–100:** Flagship priority
- **75–84:** High-priority Phase 1 asset
- **60–74:** Publish when it completes a cluster or graph relationship
- **45–59:** Hold for stronger evidence or a later phase
- **Below 45:** Reject or merge into another page

A topic with weak demand may still qualify if it is essential to the diagnostic graph. A topic with strong demand may be rejected if Rank Builder cannot add meaningful value.

## 12. Mandatory topic-approval questions

A topic may enter production only if all answers are satisfactory.

1. What exact problem is the reader trying to solve?
2. Is the intent materially distinct from an existing Rank Builder page?
3. What would make this page better than the existing search results?
4. What evidence can Rank Builder provide?
5. What visual, example, test, tool, or dataset will make the page noncommodity?
6. Which existing page will link to it?
7. Which page will it link to as the next action?
8. What could make the page misleading?
9. How quickly can the information become outdated?
10. What would justify merging or deleting the page later?

“No competitor has this exact title” is not proof of distinct intent.

## 13. Search-intent separation rules

Create separate pages only when at least one of the following is true:

- The user sees a different exact error or status.
- The diagnostic path is materially different.
- The implementation steps are materially different.
- The decision criteria are materially different.
- The target audience requires a different technical level.
- The page can support a distinct tool, dataset, or visual.
- The search result set indicates a distinct practical intent.

Do not create separate pages merely because:

- The wording differs slightly.
- The keyword is singular on one page and plural on another.
- A CMS name can be inserted into the title.
- A city, industry, or business type can be substituted.
- A year can be appended.
- A search-volume tool reports several close variants.
- A competitor has split one concept into several pages.

# Part V: Editorial identity

## 14. Voice

Rank Builder’s voice is:

- Precise
- Calm
- Skeptical
- Evidence-aware
- Technically literate
- Accessible without being patronizing
- Direct about uncertainty
- Hostile to empty marketing claims
- Willing to say that a supposed problem is harmless
- Willing to say that no guaranteed solution exists

The voice is not:

- Breathless
- Guru-like
- Corporate
- Overly cute
- Sensational
- Artificially authoritative
- Stuffed with motivational filler
- Obsessed with “hacks,” “secrets,” or “game-changing” tactics

Permitted humor should clarify or relieve tension. It must not obscure the answer.

## 15. Prohibited language

Avoid:

- “Guaranteed rankings”
- “Instant indexing”
- “Secret ranking factor”
- “Crack the algorithm”
- “Dominate Google overnight”
- “Game-changing”
- “Ultimate” unless the page is genuinely comprehensive
- “Everything you need to know” unless that claim is defensible
- “Google penalty” when no manual or algorithmic penalty is established
- “Google hates”
- “The algorithm wants”
- “SEO juice”
- “Just submit the sitemap”
- “Simply request indexing”
- “Fix your crawl budget” for ordinary small sites without evidence
- “According to experts” without naming and citing them

Do not anthropomorphize Google when a technical description is available.

## 16. Claim taxonomy

Every nontrivial claim belongs to one of five classes.

### 16.1 Documented fact

A behavior or requirement explicitly documented by an authoritative source.

Use language such as:

- “Google documents that…”
- “The HTTP specification requires…”
- “Shopify generates…”

### 16.2 Direct observation

Something Rank Builder observed in a controlled test, live inspection, or dataset.

Use language such as:

- “In our test…”
- “We observed…”
- “The tested response contained…”

Include the date, environment, and limitations.

### 16.3 Inference

A conclusion supported by evidence but not directly established.

Use language such as:

- “This suggests…”
- “The most likely explanation is…”
- “We infer…”

Never convert an inference into a documented fact during editing.

### 16.4 Recommendation

Rank Builder’s judgment about what the reader should do.

Use language such as:

- “We recommend…”
- “The safest order is…”
- “In practice, begin with…”

Recommendations must state important tradeoffs.

### 16.5 Unknown or unresolved

A question without adequate evidence.

Use language such as:

- “The available evidence does not establish…”
- “Google does not publicly specify…”
- “We cannot determine this from Search Console alone.”

An honest unknown is better than decorative certainty.

# Part VI: Evidence and sourcing

## 17. Source hierarchy

Use the strongest available source.

### Tier 1: Primary authoritative sources

Preferred for technical requirements and platform behavior:

- Google Search Central documentation
- Google Search Console Help
- Google Search status documentation
- Web standards
- Official framework documentation
- Official CMS documentation
- Official source code
- Official API documentation
- Court filings, legislation, or regulatory material when applicable
- Rank Builder’s own reproducible tests and datasets

### Tier 2: Peer-reviewed or formal research

Preferred for empirical claims about search behavior, user behavior, ranking effects, or measurement.

### Tier 3: High-quality technical secondary sources

Use when primary documentation is incomplete:

- Named engineers
- Reputable technical publications
- Detailed practitioner studies with disclosed methods
- Conference presentations with evidence

### Tier 4: Community evidence

Use for discovering symptoms, edge cases, or candidate explanations:

- GitHub issues
- Search Central Community posts
- Reddit
- Stack Overflow
- CMS forums

Community evidence must not be presented as universal behavior.

### Tier 5: Competitor content

Use only to understand search-result coverage, terminology, and missing value. A competing article is not the preferred authority for a claim that can be sourced elsewhere.

## 18. Citation rules

New work must use `citationMode: "inline-required"`.

Every listed citation must appear in the article body. Every inline citation must resolve to a listed source.

Citations are required for:

- Search-engine requirements
- Search Console behavior
- Platform behavior
- Specific time ranges
- Numerical claims
- Changes to products or reports
- Technical limitations
- Quotes
- Research findings
- Claims that a practice is recommended, deprecated, or unsupported

A paragraph may contain several sentences supported by one source. Do not attach citations mechanically to every sentence when one citation clearly supports the paragraph.

Do not cite:

- Search-result snippets
- AI-generated summaries
- Unsourced infographics
- Another article that merely cites the primary source
- A social post when an official announcement is available
- A page that was not actually opened and reviewed

## 19. Source-recency rules

Assign every article a factual-volatility class.

| Class | Examples | Mandatory review interval |
|---|---|---:|
| Stable | HTTP behavior, basic crawling concepts | 12 months |
| Moderately changing | Google documentation, canonical guidance | 6 months |
| Product-sensitive | Search Console reports, CMS interfaces | 90 days |
| Interface-sensitive | Exact menus, buttons, screenshots | 60 days |
| Current-event | Updates, incidents, announcements | 30 days |
| Experimental | Rank Builder studies and monitoring | At every new data release |

A review does not automatically require changing `revisedAt`.

The revision date changes only when the public article has materially changed. Dates must not be updated merely to make content appear fresh.

## 20. AI-assisted editorial process

Automation may assist with:

- Research discovery
- Source organization
- Outline testing
- Drafting
- Terminology consistency
- Internal-link suggestions
- Duplicate detection
- Schema adaptation
- Validation
- Mechanical publication

Automation may not be treated as evidence.

The final article must be evaluated for:

- Accuracy
- Originality
- Completeness
- Source fidelity
- Internal consistency
- Search intent
- Unsupported certainty
- Fabricated experience
- Repeated boilerplate
- Meaningful reader utility

Where readers would reasonably care how a study, test, or analysis was created, the method must be disclosed.

# Part VII: Canonical article formats

## 21. Format selection

Rank Builder’s five canonical formats remain binding.

### 21.1 Explainer

Use when the primary intent is:

- What does this mean?
- Why does this happen?
- How does the mechanism work?
- Is this actually a problem?

Required roles:

- Definition
- Mechanism
- Examples
- Boundaries

Typical Phase 1 uses:

- Page Indexing statuses
- Crawling versus indexing
- Canonical selection
- JavaScript rendering
- Crawl budget

### 21.2 Playbook

Use when the primary intent is:

- How do I diagnose this?
- How do I fix this?
- What should I do first?
- What is the correct sequence?

Required roles:

- Preconditions
- Ordered process
- Failure cases

Typical Phase 1 uses:

- Indexing diagnostic flow
- WordPress accidental noindex repair
- Redirect-error diagnosis
- Search Console validation workflow

### 21.3 Claim check

Use when the primary intent is evaluating a common assertion.

Required roles:

- Identified claim
- Sources and evidence
- Conclusion
- Limitations

Typical Phase 1 uses:

- Does requesting indexing make Google index a page?
- Does a sitemap guarantee indexing?

### 21.4 Data note

Use for original or external datasets and experiments.

Required roles:

- Dataset and period
- Methodology
- Result
- Limitations

Typical Phase 1 uses:

- Google Indexing Time Study

### 21.5 Checklist

Use where completion can be meaningfully verified.

Required roles:

- Checklist
- Completion criteria

Typical Phase 1 uses:

- Pre-indexing technical check

- Before-requesting-indexing checklist

# Part VIII: Page construction

## 23. Universal page anatomy

Every Phase 1 article must contain the following elements.

### 23.1 Direct answer

The page opens with a direct answer of approximately 40–100 words.

It must:

- Name the condition
- Explain whether it is necessarily a problem
- State the first verification step
- Avoid withholding the answer to force scrolling

### 23.2 Diagnostic context

Explain what evidence establishes the condition and what evidence does not.

### 23.3 Mechanism

Explain the underlying search, HTTP, platform, or rendering behavior.

### 23.4 Verification

Give the reader specific checks.

### 23.5 Causes in meaningful order

Order causes by:

1. Probability
2. Ease of confirmation
3. Severity
4. Cost of intervention

Do not present an alphabetical dump.

### 23.6 Corrective action

State the safest fix or next action for each confirmed cause.

### 23.7 Failure cases

Explain:

- What a failed fix looks like
- What should not be changed
- When the reader should wait
- When a developer is required
- When evidence remains inconclusive

### 23.8 Boundaries

State what the page cannot promise.

### 23.9 Next diagnostic step

Link to the most useful next page, tool, or checklist.

Titles must be:

- Descriptive
- Specific
- Concise enough to scan
- Aligned with the searcher’s language
- Honest about the page’s scope
- Distinct from nearby Rank Builder pages

Preferred patterns include:

- `Crawled – Currently Not Indexed: What It Means and What to Fix`
- `Redirect Error in Search Console: How to Find the Failed Hop`
- `How Long Does Google Take to Index a Page?`
- `Does Requesting Indexing Make Google Index a Page?`

Avoid:

- Front-loading the brand name
- Repeating several keyword variants
- Adding the current year without a real annual edition
- Fake urgency
- Unnecessary superlatives
- Withholding the actual subject

## 26. Description rules

The description should:

- Summarize the whole page
- Identify the user’s problem
- Explain what the page helps them determine or do
- Distinguish the page from neighboring articles
- Avoid generic promotional language

A working range of roughly 140–175 characters is useful for editorial consistency, but it is not a hard search-engine limit.

## 27. Direct-answer rules

The `directAnswer` field and visible introduction must agree.

A direct answer should not:

- Promise guaranteed indexing
- Confuse indexing with ranking
- Treat a Search Console label as a complete diagnosis
- Recommend a fix before confirming the cause
- Say “wait” without explaining what should be monitored
- Say “improve quality” without defining what evidence suggests a content-selection issue

# Part IX: Noncommodity value

## 28. The two-value rule

No Phase 1 page may be published unless it provides at least two forms of noncommodity value.

Qualifying value includes:

- Original decision tree
- Original SVG diagram
- Tested command
- Tested configuration
- Real output example
- Redacted Search Console example
- HTTP-header example
- Redirect trace
- Platform-specific procedure
- Downloadable checklist
- Downloadable template
- Data table
- Original experiment
- Original dataset
- Interactive tool
- Comparison against official documentation
- Explicit edge-case analysis
- A ranked diagnostic sequence

A generic introduction, a list of definitions, and citations do not satisfy this rule by themselves.

## 29. Visual standards

Phase 1 visuals should be functional rather than decorative.

Preferred assets:

- Diagnostic flowcharts
- Pipeline diagrams
- Redirect paths
- Canonical clusters
- Before-and-after examples
- Search Console status maps
- HTTP response diagrams
- Controlled-test timelines
- Original charts
- Platform setting diagrams

Each image must have:

- Accurate dimensions
- Useful alt text
- Caption
- Credit
- Rights status
- Registered media record
- Legible mobile rendering

Important flagship, research, and timely pages should receive a unique large image suitable for social previews and Discover.

Do not create large images by stretching small screenshots or putting a sentence on an otherwise empty background.

## 30. Screenshot standards

Screenshots may be used when the visual interface is essential.

They must:

- Come from an owned, authorized, or public test environment
- Remove private property names, account identifiers, email addresses, and sensitive queries
- Show enough interface context to be understandable
- Include the capture date where product volatility matters
- Be replaced when the interface materially changes
- Avoid implying that one screenshot proves universal platform behavior

Where possible, convert an interface-dependent explanation into a durable diagram supplemented by a screenshot.

Every tool must have an indexable landing page that explains:

- What the tool checks
- What it does not check
- Required input
- Privacy behavior
- A sample result
- How to interpret each output
- Common failure modes
- Related articles
- Limitations
- Last material update

The Phase 1 tools are:

1. **Indexability Inspector**
2. **Redirect Chain Visualizer**

Tools must:

- Perform the promised function
- Return useful results without fake progress
- Avoid mandatory account creation
- Avoid collecting unnecessary user data
- Avoid deceptive ad placement
- Provide readable error messages
- Link output conditions to relevant Rank Builder guides

## 32. Indexability Inspector scope

The first version should inspect:

- Submitted URL
- Redirect chain
- Final status
- Content type
- `robots.txt` accessibility
- Meta robots directives
- `X-Robots-Tag`
- Declared canonical
- Canonical target status
- Title
- Main heading

It must not claim to know:

Output should link to:

4. First crawl

# Part XI: Internal linking

## 36. Diagnostic graph model

The indexing cluster must behave like a connected diagnostic system.

Every page should consider five relationship types.

| Relationship | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Parent | Broader hub or diagnostic system |
| Prerequisite | Concept the reader must understand first |
| Alternative diagnosis | Similar symptom with a different cause |
| Corrective action | Guide or tool used to resolve the issue |
| Escalation | Next step when the current page cannot resolve it |

Example for `Crawled – Currently Not Indexed`:

- Parent: Google Indexing Troubleshooting
- Prerequisite: Crawling vs. Indexing vs. Ranking
- Alternative diagnosis: Discovered – Currently Not Indexed
- Corrective action: URL Inspection Guide

# Part XIII: Production workflow

## 46. Stage 1: Opportunity packet

Before drafting, create an opportunity packet containing:

- Working title
- Primary query
- Secondary query variants
- Searcher state
- Proposed canonical format
- Topic score
- Existing Rank Builder overlap
- Competing pages
- Missing value in current results
- Proposed unique value
- Required sources
- Required visual or tool
- Planned inbound links
- Planned outbound internal links
- Volatility class
- Update requirement

No article begins from a title alone.

## 47. Stage 2: Research brief

The research brief must include:

- Exact question being answered
- Definitions
- Primary source excerpts or notes
- Platform-specific documentation
- Contradictions between sources
- Unknowns
- Important edge cases
- Claims that must be qualified
- Test opportunities
- Proposed structure
- Citation IDs
- Date accessed

Research notes must separate source language from editorial conclusions.

## 48. Stage 3: Editorial outline

The outline must identify:

- Direct answer
- Primary article role
- Required canonical-format sections
- Diagnostic order
- Visual placement
- Examples
- Internal-link placement
- Limitations
- Completion criteria

The outline is approved before full drafting for flagship, research, tool, and platform pages.

## 49. Stage 4: Drafting

ChatGPT writes the exact substantive text.

The draft must:

- Use the approved sources
- Preserve claim boundaries
- Avoid unsupported platform assumptions
- Include the planned original value
- Use descriptive headings
- Contain stable anchor-ready sections
- Avoid raw HTML and unsupported Markdown
- Follow the canonical format
- Include complete frontmatter

Codex may not “improve,” “expand,” or “SEO optimize” prose without exact replacement language from the editor-in-chief.

## 50. Stage 5: Editorial review

Every draft receives four review lenses.

### Intent review

- Does it solve the intended problem?
- Is it distinct from existing pages?
- Is the direct answer immediate?

### Evidence review

- Does every important claim have support?
- Are inferences labeled?
- Were all sources actually reviewed?
- Are dates and UI details current?

### Technical review

- Are commands and instructions correct?
- Do examples behave as claimed?
- Are HTTP, canonical, robots, and rendering distinctions accurate?

### Reader review

- Can a nonexpert follow the diagnostic sequence?
- Are important terms defined?
- Does the page tell the reader what to do next?
- Does it avoid unnecessary detours?

## 51. Stage 6: Internal-link review

Before publication:

1. Identify at least one existing page that will link to the new page.
2. Identify the new page’s parent.
3. Identify the most likely alternative diagnosis.
4. Identify the corrective-action page or tool.
5. Confirm all paths are canonical.
6. Confirm anchor text is descriptive.
7. Update the hub.
8. Check for cannibalization.

A page may not enter `published` state with no planned inbound path.

## 52. Stage 7: Mechanical integration

Codex may:

- Create the file
- Insert approved frontmatter and prose
- Register approved media
- Add approved registry entries
- Add approved internal-link metadata
- Run format conversion
- Run compilation
- Run tests
- Build production output
- Produce a diff
- Prepare the release

Codex may not:

- Rewrite arguments
- Invent citations
- Choose new topics
- Add unsupported sources
- Change page intent
- Create new claims
- Remove limitations
- Add a commercial CTA
- Expand scope
- Create unapproved platform variants

## 53. Stage 8: Validation

Every batch must run:

```text
npm run content:check
npm run typecheck
npm run lint
npm test
npm run build:pages
npm run verify
npm run content:links
npm run release:review
git diff --check
```

Additional review must check:

- Built route existence
- Unique title and H1
- Canonical URL
- Index/follow controls
- Sitemap inclusion
- Feed inclusion where applicable
- Structured data
- Source links
- Internal links
- Registered media
- Mobile overflow
- Desktop rendering
- Browser console
- Failed resources
- Request budget

No failed deterministic gate is dismissed as “probably fine.”

## 54. Stage 9: Release

Release batches should normally contain six to eight pages.

The release must use:

- A clean source checkout
- One reviewed commit chain
- Exact GitHub-main alignment
- The established Cloudflare release path
- Immutable deployment verification
- Apex verification
- Rollback readiness

Do not create one work order per trivial editorial file. The batch is the governed unit unless an isolated blocker genuinely requires separation.

## 55. Stage 10: Postpublication checks

### Within 24 hours

- Confirm route returns 200.
- Confirm canonical and indexing controls.
- Confirm sitemap and feed.
- Confirm analytics receipt.
- Confirm no browser failures.
- Confirm internal links.

### Within seven days

- Check URL Inspection.
- Check whether Google discovered or crawled the page.
- Review Search Console’s recent data.
- Confirm query and page dimensions are captured.
- Check whether the title is being rewritten unexpectedly.

### At thirty days

- Review impressions
- Review query coverage
- Review index state
- Review average position distribution
- Check for competing Rank Builder pages
- Improve weak snippets or internal links where evidence supports it

### At sixty to ninety days

Choose one:

- Maintain
- Expand
- Retarget
- Merge
- Redirect
- Archive
- Promote into a hub
- Build supporting pages

## 60. Phase 1 management targets

These are operational targets, not forecasts or guarantees.

By the end of Phase 1:

- At least thirty-five new public assets should be complete.
- Every asset should have an inbound link.
- No public asset should be orphaned.
- At least twenty-five pages should receive Search Console impressions.
- At least ten pages should rank within the top fifty for one or more relevant queries.
- At least three pages should reach the top thirty.
- Sitewide daily impressions should materially exceed the July baseline.
- At least one tool or research asset should earn an unsolicited external citation, link, or meaningful community reference.
- Query- and page-level analytics should support the Phase 2 plan.

A useful directional target is a fourfold increase in daily impressions by the middle of the phase and a tenfold increase by the end. These are planning thresholds, not promises.

## 61. Page action rules

### Page receives impressions at positions 8–30

- Compare the page with the dominant intent.
- Improve title clarity.
- Improve direct answer.
- Add missing examples or evidence.
- Strengthen internal links.
- Add a tool or visual if absent.
- Avoid unnecessary URL changes.

### Page receives impressions but almost no clicks

- Review title and snippet.
- Check whether the title promises the correct outcome.
- Check whether the query is informationally satisfied on the results page.
- Add a reason to visit through utility, not withheld information.
- Confirm the ranking page actually matches the query.

### Page receives no meaningful impressions after 60–90 days

Determine whether it:

- Supports the diagnostic graph
- Targets a real query
- Is indexed
- Has inbound links
- Duplicates another page
- Needs retargeting
- Should be merged
- Should remain as useful support
- Should be archived

### Several pages rank for the same query

- Choose the strongest canonical intent.
- Merge unique value.
- Redirect the weaker page when appropriate.
- Update internal links.
- Do not keep both merely because both are indexed.

# Part XVI: Search and AI visibility

## 62. AI features

Rank Builder will not create a separate layer of “AI Overview optimization.”

The same foundational SEO practices apply. Pages must be indexable and eligible to appear with a snippet.

Rank Builder should:

- Use precise terminology
- Answer subquestions explicitly
- Keep important content in text
- Use clear headings
- Provide primary evidence
- Publish original diagrams and datasets
- Connect related subtopics
- Maintain accurate structured data
- Avoid creating fake AI-specific files or schema

The best defense against answer summarization is not hiding the answer. It is providing evidence, tools, examples, and diagnostic depth that remain useful after the summary.

## 63. Discover eligibility

Discover is an upside channel, not the foundation of Phase 1 forecasting.

Pages most suitable for Discover include:

- Original research
- Major Google changes
- Timely Search Console changes
- Search incidents
- Strong visual explainers
- Counterintuitive findings
- New tool launches

These pages need:

- A compelling but accurate title
- A unique large image
- A clear story
- Timeliness or original insight
- No clickbait
- No withheld crucial fact
- `max-image-preview:large`

# Part XVIII: Editorial integrity

## 68. Absolute prohibitions

Rank Builder must never publish:

- Fabricated test results
- Fabricated Search Console screenshots
- Fabricated case studies
- Invented clients
- Invented credentials
- Invented quotations
- Invented publication dates
- Fake expert biographies
- Unsupported claims of ranking causation
- Guaranteed outcomes
- Confidential user or client data
- Private analytics without authorization
- Copied proprietary datasets
- Disguised advertisements
- Functionality that does not work

## 69. Corrections

When a material error is discovered:

1. Correct the public content.
2. Update `revisedAt`.
3. Add a correction-history entry.
4. State what was wrong.
5. State what changed.
6. Preserve the original publication date.
7. Revalidate affected links and claims.
8. Reinspect dependent pages.

Minor spelling and formatting corrections do not require public correction entries unless they changed meaning.

## 70. Commercial separation

Phase 1 diagnostic pages should not become aggressive service funnels.

Primary calls to action should be:

- Run a tool
- Continue diagnosis
- Download a checklist
- Read the methodology
- Inspect a related condition
- Review a source
- Subscribe to updates if such a system is later approved

Commercial relevance comes from trust and audience quality, not from interrupting every technical explanation with a sales pitch.

Buyer-defense articles may discuss procurement and provider evaluation directly. Technical diagnostics should remain editorially independent.

# Part XX: Final operating doctrine

Rank Builder Phase 1 follows ten rules.

1. **Own one useful territory before expanding.**
2. **Publish distinct solutions, not keyword permutations.**
3. **Answer immediately, then provide depth.**
4. **Prefer primary evidence.**
5. **Separate documentation, observation, inference, and recommendation.**
6. **Require noncommodity value.**
7. **Build a diagnostic graph rather than an article pile.**
8. **Give every flagship asset a distribution purpose.**
9. **Use performance evidence to expand, merge, or stop.**
10. **Never let production volume outrun editorial judgment.**

The Phase 1 goal is not to appear busy. It is to transform Rank Builder from a technically sound small publication into the most useful independent destination for diagnosing Google indexing and Search Console problems.

Everything published during this phase must contribute to that outcome.
