## Identified claim

GEO is a useful label for a new measurement problem: whether brands appear in generated answers. It is a much weaker claim when sold as a secret replacement for SEO. Google’s current documentation says AI Mode and AI Overviews require no special markup, no AI file, and no separate technical optimization system.

**GEO does not replace SEO.**

Google’s current documentation says the same foundational SEO practices remain relevant for AI Overviews and AI Mode and that there are **no additional technical requirements** for inclusion.[@rb-handoff-20-08-source-1]

Google also says you do not need:

- special AI schema;
- a new AI text file;
- a separate machine-readable optimization file;
- pages for every fan-out query.

So what is GEO?

**Generative Engine Optimization** is a reasonable industry label for the work of:

- measuring brand visibility in generated answers;
- improving source clarity;
- creating original information worth citing;
- making content easy to retrieve;
- tracking AI citations across engines.

It becomes nonsense when sold as:

> We know the secret markup that makes Google AI cite you.

Google says there is no such special requirement.

**Why “GEO vs SEO” is hot.**

Google itself published a new generative AI optimization resource in May 2026 and specifically called out misconceptions around “AEO/GEO.”[@rb-handoff-20-08-source-2]

At the same time, Google says AI Mode has more than one billion monthly active users and AI search is increasing query volume.[@rb-handoff-20-08-source-3]

Marketers now have a real problem:

```text
How do we remain visible when the answer is synthesized?
```

A large consulting industry has naturally responded by inventing as many new acronyms as human civilization can bear.

**What SEO covers.**

SEO includes:

- crawling;
- indexing;
- canonicalization;
- internal linking;
- site architecture;
- page experience;
- content;
- structured data;
- images;
- video;
- local entities;
- shopping feeds;
- authority and reputation;
- measurement.

Those things remain necessary.

Google says a page used as a supporting link in AI features must be indexed and eligible to appear with a snippet in ordinary Search.[@rb-handoff-20-08-source-1]

That is SEO.

**What GEO adds.**

The new layer is the **response surface**.

Classic measurement:

```text
query
→ ranking
→ impression
→ click
```

Generative measurement:

```text
prompt
→ hidden fan-out searches
→ generated answer
→ citations / links / entities
→ optional click
→ follow-up prompt
```

That creates new questions.

- Was the brand mentioned?
- Was the page cited?
- Was the claim accurate?
- Which competitors were cited?
- Did the citation persist across repeat runs?
- Did the answer change after a follow-up?
- Did visibility lead to branded search or conversion?

Those are legitimate GEO questions.

**The fan-out misunderstanding.**

Google explains that AI Mode and AI Overviews may issue multiple related searches across subtopics.[@rb-handoff-20-08-source-1]

Some vendors interpret this as:

> Create one page for every possible subquery.

Google’s 2026 optimization guide warns against doing exactly that when the goal is manipulation.[@rb-handoff-20-08-source-4]

Scaled pages around query variations can violate scaled content abuse policy.

The better approach is one strong resource that covers the real information need.

**No special AI schema.**

Google says there is no special schema.org markup required for AI features.[@rb-handoff-20-08-source-1]

Use supported structured data because it accurately describes visible content.

Examples:

- Product;
- Organization;
- LocalBusiness;
- Article;
- VideoObject;
- Event;
- Recipe.

Do not invent:

```text
GEOScore
AICitation
LLMReady
AIOptimized
```

and then sell the validator.

**No special AI file.**

Google says you do not need new machine-readable files or AI text files to appear in its AI features.[@rb-handoff-20-08-source-1]

That does not mean every other platform ignores every emerging convention.

It means:

> Do not sell a Google ranking guarantee based on a file Google says it does not require.

**Information gain matters.**

The strongest overlap between good SEO and useful GEO is originality.

A generated answer has little reason to cite the 47th generic definition page.

It has more reason to retrieve a page containing:

- official document;
- original data;
- test;
- calculator;
- real comparison;
- expert explanation;
- field evidence;
- current price;
- unique image;
- primary-source synthesis.

Google’s 2026 guide emphasizes valuable, unique, non-commodity content.[@rb-handoff-20-08-source-4]

That is not an acronym.

It is editorial work.

**Entity consistency matters.**

AI answers synthesize information from multiple systems.

Keep consistent:

```text
brand name
legal name
product names
people
locations
dates
pricing
features
business profile
merchant feed
organization markup
```

If your homepage says one thing, Business Profile says another, and five directories contain old information, the machine has several opportunities to misunderstand you.

**GEO tools: what they can measure.**

A useful tool can sample:

- prompt sets;
- engines;
- citations;
- mentions;
- competitors;
- answer sentiment;
- answer changes.

But ask:

- how many runs?
- which location?
- logged in or logged out?
- which model?
- which date?
- which prompt set?
- how variable are results?
- can raw observations be exported?

Do not treat one generated answer as a stable ranking.

**How to hire a GEO agency.**

Ask five questions.

**1. What do you do that is not SEO?**

A strong answer might include generative visibility measurement and prompt-journey analysis.

A weak answer is:

> We add AI schema.

**2. Which platforms?**

Google AI Mode is not ChatGPT.

ChatGPT is not Perplexity.

Bing is not Gemini.

Define scope.

**3. How is success measured?**

Require:

- prompt set;
- observation cadence;
- citations;
- brand mentions;
- business outcome.

**4. What claims are guaranteed?**

No vendor can guarantee a Google AI citation.

**5. What original information will we create?**

If the answer is none, the plan may simply be renamed SEO reporting.

**A sane 2026 operating model.**

Use:

```text
SEO foundation
+
original content
+
entity consistency
+
structured commerce/local data
+
AI visibility measurement
=
modern search strategy
```

Call the last layer GEO if that helps teams organize work.

Do not use the new label to abandon the old foundation.

## Conclusion

**FAQ.**

**Does Google recognize GEO as a separate ranking system?**

Google’s current guidance says normal SEO best practices remain foundational and no special AI optimization is required.

**Do I need llms.txt for Google AI Mode?**

Google says no new AI text file is required for inclusion in its Search AI features.

**Is GEO useless?**

No. Measuring generative mentions and citations is a legitimate new discipline. The hype begins when measurement is sold as a secret ranking mechanism.

**Are AI Mode citations the same as organic rankings?**

No. AI systems can retrieve sources through different processes and query fan-out. The visibility surface is different.

**Should I hire a GEO agency?**

Only if the agency can explain its methodology, platform scope, measurement, limitations, and what useful information it will create.

**Verdict.**

GEO is real as a **measurement and visibility problem**.

It is mostly marketing when presented as a replacement for SEO or a hidden technical protocol.

If a GEO vendor cannot explain crawling, indexing, content, entities, measurement, and uncertainty, it has invented a new acronym faster than it has learned Search.

**Conclusion in brief.**

GEO is a useful label for a new measurement problem: whether brands appear in generated answers. It is a much weaker claim when sold as a secret replacement for SEO. Google’s current documentation says AI Mode and AI Overviews require no special markup, no AI file, and no separate technical optimization system.
