## Identified claim

Hibu is a real, established digital-marketing company, but its own minimum-term and phone-cancellation rules plus a substantial public complaint record justify unusually strict contract and account-ownership controls.

**Verdict.**

**Hibu is a legitimate operating company, not a company we can responsibly label a proven scam. The buyer risk is still high when a small business signs a bundled minimum-term package without independently verifying deliverables, cancellation dates, account ownership, and reporting access.**

**Why this audit needs restraint.**

Hibu belongs in a different evidentiary category from companies that were convicted, permanently enjoined, or placed under a final deceptive-practices order.

The reviewed sources establish that Hibu is an operating digital-marketing company with published terms, a headquarters business profile, and a broad product line. They do **not** establish that Hibu as a whole is a criminal or adjudicated fraudulent enterprise.

What the sources do show is a combination that should make a small-business buyer slow down:

- multi-product minimum terms;
- cancellation windows tied to service months;
- telephone cancellation requirements for many services;
- potential early-termination charges;
- automatic month-to-month continuation after some minimum terms;
- public complaints alleging sales, performance, billing, cancellation, website, and account-control disputes.

Those signals do not prove every complaint. They do make contract diligence mandatory.

## Sources and evidence

**What Hibu sells.**

Hibu’s published terms cover websites, paid search, display advertising, social campaigns, reviews, listings, reputation products, and related packages.

That breadth creates one practical risk: a buyer may think it purchased “SEO” while the order actually combines several products with different start dates, minimum terms, budgets, and cancellation rules.

A website can start on one date. Paid search can start when the campaign is ready. Display or social minimum terms can begin when those campaigns go live. The cancellation date for one product may not end another product.

The buyer should therefore demand a one-page order matrix:

| Product | Monthly fee | Ad budget | Start trigger | Minimum term | Cancellation deadline | Early termination fee |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|---:|
| Website | Record | N/A | Order or launch | Record | Record | Record |
| SEO/local ranking | Record | N/A | Record | Record | Record | Record |
| Paid search | Record | Record | Campaign launch | Record | Record | Record |
| Display | Record | Record | Campaign launch | Record | Record | Record |
| Social | Record | Record | Campaign launch | Record | Record | Record |

Without that table, “six months” can become several overlapping clocks.

**What Hibu’s own terms say.**

Hibu’s general terms provide a seven-day review period for some telephone-assisted orders. After that period, cancellation depends on the applicable minimum term and service-specific rules.

For many services, Hibu’s published terms require cancellation by telephone and require notice at least fifteen days before the end of the minimum term or current service month. Missing the window can extend service for another month. Some services also permit early-termination fees.

The paid-search terms can impose an early-termination fee of $399 or the monthly budget, whichever is greater, under specified conditions. Other products use different amounts or structures.

These are not hidden merely because they are unfavorable. They are published contractual terms. The buyer’s problem begins when the sales explanation, order form, and full terms do not produce one understandable obligation.

**What the complaint record shows.**

The Better Business Bureau complaint page reviewed for Hibu displayed 109 complaints and categorized many as order, service, product, billing, and sales issues. Recent complainants alleged disputes involving:

- unfinished or inaccurate websites;
- AI-generated errors;
- campaigns they believed were not delivered;
- weak or unavailable reporting;
- cancellation requests that did not stop billing;
- misunderstandings about separate product terms;
- business-profile or phone-number control;
- services continuing through a minimum term.

Hibu responded to complaints and disputed many allegations. Some complaints were resolved to the customer’s satisfaction. Others were answered without customer acceptance.

That mixed record matters. A complaint is not a judicial finding. A repeated category can still reveal where buyers need stronger controls.

The strongest pattern is not “Hibu never does work.” The stronger, supportable conclusion is:

> Buyers repeatedly report mismatched expectations about scope, launch, performance, cancellation, and billing, while Hibu repeatedly relies on the signed agreement and product-specific terms.

That is a contract-governance warning.

**Buyer-control checklist.**

- Get the exact legal entity, product name, and salesperson promises in writing.
- Require a complete statement of work with measurable deliverables, owners, dates, and exclusions.
- Keep the domain, DNS, website, analytics, Search Console, ad accounts, and business profiles under buyer-controlled administration.
- Reject ranking, revenue, lead-quality, or income guarantees that cannot be substantiated.
- Require contract, renewal, cancellation, refund, and early-termination terms before payment.
- Preserve screenshots, call notes, invoices, campaign exports, and change records.
- Test a limited scope before granting broad access or signing a long minimum term.
- Define offboarding, data export, credential revocation, and content ownership before launch.

## Conclusion

**SEO proof questions.**

Before buying a Hibu SEO or local-ranking product, require written answers to:

1. Which website will be optimized?
2. Who owns the domain and final source files?
3. Will the buyer retain Search Console owner access?
4. Which pages will be created or changed?
5. How many content deliverables are included?
6. What counts as a completed optimization?
7. Are backlinks promised, and how are they documented?
8. Which reports show work rather than only outcomes?
9. Who controls the Google Business Profile?
10. What happens to the website and reviews after cancellation?

Do not accept “dashboard access” as a substitute for deliverable evidence.

**The account-ownership problem.**

A small business should remain the primary owner of:

- domain registration;
- DNS;
- Google Business Profile;
- Search Console;
- GA4;
- Google Ads;
- Meta assets;
- call-tracking numbers where portability is required;
- website content and media.

If a vendor-controlled phone number becomes the public identity on a business profile, offboarding can become a continuity problem. If a vendor-built website exists only on a vendor platform, the buyer needs an export or transition clause before launch.

**Who may still be a fit.**

Hibu may fit a business that:

- wants one vendor for several channels;
- accepts a structured minimum term;
- understands that paid media, website work, listings, and SEO are separate services;
- has an internal owner who will review every launch;
- keeps independent analytics and account access;
- can verify monthly deliverables.

It is a poor fit for a business that:

- expects month-to-month flexibility;
- will not read service-specific terms;
- needs bespoke technical SEO;
- cannot independently audit work;
- expects guaranteed leads or rankings;
- cannot tolerate a difficult transition.

**Bottom line.**

Hibu is real. That is not the same as saying every package is a good purchase.

The public record supports a high-diligence verdict: read every product term, record every start date, preserve account ownership, define deliverables, and calendar the cancellation window before the first payment.
