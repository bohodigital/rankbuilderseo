## Dataset and period

AI Overviews clearly change click behavior, but the headline numbers are not directly interchangeable. Pew measured browsing sessions, Ahrefs compared ranking-page CTR across keyword sets, and Seer analyzed client search data with its own methodology.

AI Overviews are associated with lower click-through behavior in several independent datasets.

But there is no single universal number for:

```text
AI Overviews reduce clicks by X%
```

because different studies measure different populations, queries, positions, devices, and outcomes.

Three commonly cited findings illustrate the problem.

**Pew Research Center.**

In a study based on browsing activity from 900 U.S. adults, Pew found users clicked a traditional search result on about **8%** of visits when an AI summary appeared, versus **15%** when no AI summary appeared.

Clicks on links inside the AI summary itself occurred in about **1%** of visits with an AI summary.

**Ahrefs.**

Ahrefs reported that the presence of an AI Overview correlated with roughly a **58% lower average CTR for the top-ranking page** in its updated study.

This is not the same metric as Pew’s share of search visits producing a click.

**Seer Interactive.**

Data reported by Search Engine Land showed AI Overview CTR rising from a low point in late 2025 into early 2026.

The reported comparison included roughly:

- no AI Overview: 3.3% CTR;
- cited in AI Overview: 2.1%;
- not cited: 0.9%.

Different dataset.

Different denominator.

Different conclusion.

**Why these studies are not actually contradictory.**

Suppose one study asks:

> What percentage of search visits led to any result click?

Another asks:

> What is the CTR of the page ranking first?

Another asks:

> What is the CTR for pages cited in AI Overviews versus pages not cited?

All three can be correct simultaneously.

The web loves one number because one number fits into a screenshot.

Measurement is crueler.

**Pew’s methodology.**

Pew analyzed web browsing data from 900 U.S. adults for March 2025.

The broader search dataset contained tens of thousands of Google searches.

Pew compared what people did after search pages with and without AI-generated summaries.

Key finding:

Users were less likely to click result links when an AI summary appeared.

Pew also found longer and question-like queries were more likely to trigger AI summaries.

That matters because query type is a confounder.

Informational questions already behave differently from branded navigation.

## Methodology

**Ahrefs methodology.**

Ahrefs selected large keyword sets with and without AI Overviews and compared estimated or observed click-through behavior for ranking pages according to its published methodology.

Its updated study reported a large negative correlation for the top-ranking page.

This is especially relevant for publishers whose old model was:

```text
rank #1
→ expect large traffic share
```

AI Overviews can break that historical relationship.

**Seer’s different angle.**

Seer’s reported data suggested cited pages perform better than uncited pages on AI Overview SERPs.

That does not mean cited pages outperform equivalent rankings on clean SERPs.

The reported numbers still showed stronger CTR where no AI Overview appeared.

So two statements can both be true:

1. AI Overviews reduce overall click opportunity.
2. If an AI Overview exists, getting cited can improve your relative click opportunity.

That distinction is strategically important.

**Google’s position.**

Google has said that clicks from search pages with AI Overviews can be higher quality, meaning users may spend more time on destination sites.

Google also says generative AI features increase search usage.

Those statements concern:

- engagement quality;
- query volume;
- user behavior.

They do not automatically rebut every study showing lower click-through rates.

More searches can coexist with lower CTR per search.

**Stop using position as the only KPI.**

A page can remain:

```text
average position: 1.8
```

while clicks decline.

Track:

- impressions;
- clicks;
- CTR;
- AI feature exposure;
- citations;
- landing sessions;
- conversions;
- branded demand.

If impressions stay stable and CTR falls, SERP presentation may be changing even when ranking holds.

## Result

**Build a clean before-and-after cohort.**

For your own site:

1. Select queries that consistently show AI Overviews.
2. Select comparable queries without AI Overviews.
3. Match intent where possible.
4. Record rank.
5. Record device.
6. Record date.
7. compare CTR.
8. compare conversion.

Do not compare:

```text
branded navigational query
```

with:

```text
ten-word medical question
```

and call the difference an AI effect.

**Citation matters.**

If your page is cited in the AI Overview, track it separately.

The Seer-reported data suggests citation can improve relative CTR within AI Overview SERPs.

Use:

```text
AIO + cited
AIO + not cited
No AIO
```

as three buckets.

That is more useful than:

```text
AI / not AI
```

**Industry differences matter.**

Ahrefs’ broader 2026 CTR benchmarks show significant variation by industry.

Answer-heavy niches can suffer more zero-click behavior.

Transactional searches can retain more click pressure because the user still needs:

- product;
- booking;
- purchase;
- login;
- quote.

Do not import a publisher CTR benchmark into ecommerce planning.

**What to report to clients.**

Good:

> In our tracked query cohort, AI Overview queries produced 1.2% CTR versus 2.4% for comparable non-AI queries during July.

Weak:

> AI reduces traffic by 58%.

The second statement imports someone else’s methodology into your client’s business.

Use external research as context.

Use first-party data for decisions.

## Limitations

**FAQ.**

**Do AI Overviews reduce clicks?**

Several independent studies have found lower click behavior when AI summaries or Overviews appear.

**Is the reduction exactly 58%?**

No. That is a finding from a specific Ahrefs methodology, not a universal constant.

**Does being cited help?**

Some research reported higher CTR for cited pages than uncited pages on AI Overview SERPs.

**Does Google say clicks are better?**

Google has said clicks from AI Overview search pages can be higher quality by engagement measures.

**Can search usage increase while website CTR falls?**

Yes. More total searches and lower click rate per search can coexist.

**Measurement checklist.**

- Query intent segmented.
- AI Overview presence recorded.
- Citation status recorded.
- Rank recorded.
- Device recorded.
- CTR measured.
- Sessions measured.
- Conversion measured.
- External benchmarks labeled.
- No universal reduction claimed.
- Time period explicit.

**Verdict.**

The serious conclusion is not “AI Overviews cut clicks by exactly 58%.”

The serious conclusion is that **ranking position no longer predicts click opportunity as reliably as it once did**.

Measure the search surface, the citation state, and the business outcome together.

**Verification record.**

- Pew methodology and click figures were checked.
- Ahrefs’ 58% result is explicitly attributed to its study methodology.
- Seer figures are attributed through Search Engine Land.
