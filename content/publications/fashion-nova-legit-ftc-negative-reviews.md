## Identified claim

Fashion Nova is a real online retailer. It is also subject to a final FTC order after the agency alleged that the company suppressed hundreds of thousands of product reviews rated below four stars. In 2025, the FTC sent nearly $2.4 million to 148,351 eligible customers.

Fashion Nova is a legitimate operating fashion retailer with a serious final regulatory record.

In March 2022, the FTC finalized an order resolving allegations that Fashion Nova blocked product reviews with ratings below four stars from appearing on its website. The order required $4.2 million and prohibited review suppression and related misrepresentations.

In January 2025, the FTC announced nearly $2.4 million in payments to 148,351 eligible customers.

**How the moderation system worked.**

The FTC alleged that Fashion Nova used a third-party review-management interface. Four- and five-star reviews posted automatically while lower-star reviews were held for approval. The agency said hundreds of thousands of lower-star reviews were not approved and published.

A moderation queue is not inherently deceptive. Companies need neutral rules for spam, threats, obscenity, personal information, irrelevant material, and fraudulent submissions. The problem is using rating value as the publication rule.

**Why selective publication changes the average.**

A seller does not need to invent every review to distort the rating. If low-star submissions remain hidden while high-star submissions appear, the visible average rises and the complaint pattern disappears.

That can change click-through rate, conversion, return expectations, and the apparent consensus around a product. The remaining reviews may be genuine while the displayed population is still biased.

**What the final order requires.**

The order prohibits Fashion Nova from misrepresenting customer reviews or endorsements and from suppressing reviews because they are negative. Neutral moderation remains possible for content unrelated to the product or containing prohibited material.

The operational control is viewpoint neutrality: the same rule should apply to praise and criticism.

**The refund program.**

The FTC later created a claims process and distributed nearly $2.4 million. The 2025 notice said payments went to consumers who bought products before November 21, 2019 and filed valid claims by the deadline.

The claims window is closed. A current article should not tell readers they can still submit a claim.

**How shoppers should evaluate Fashion Nova today.**

Compare product reviews on the seller site with independent review sources, recent delivery reports, returns experience, material and sizing details, identifiable purchaser posts, and customer-service patterns.

The historical order does not prove that every current product or review is defective. It does establish why seller-controlled ratings should not be the only evidence source.

**How ecommerce companies should configure moderation.**

Publish content-based moderation rules, keep an audit log, record the rejection reason, permit appeals, and monitor rejection rates by star value.

Structured data must match visible, supported content. Do not mark up hidden reviews or calculate an aggregate from a different population than the page displays.

**Buyer checklist.**

- Use viewpoint-neutral moderation rules
- Log every rejection reason
- Compare rejection rates by star value
- Permit reviewer appeals
- Disclose incentives and material connections
- Keep structured data aligned with visible content
- Retain submitted review evidence
- Do not hide criticism because it reduces conversion
- Audit third-party review software settings
- Publish a clear review policy

The FTC alleged that hundreds of thousands of lower-star reviews were suppressed. It did not claim every negative review was hidden.

**Can a company moderate reviews?**

Yes, under neutral rules for spam, abuse, privacy, irrelevance, and similar issues.

**Are refunds still available?**

The FTC says the claims deadline passed and available funds were distributed.

No. Current practices require current evidence.
