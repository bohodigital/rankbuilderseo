## Identified claim

Roca Labs is tied to one of the strongest online-review enforcement records in this batch. A federal court granted summary judgment for the FTC in a case involving unsupported weight-loss claims, undisclosed promotional relationships, and threats against customers who posted negative reviews.

Roca Labs should not be treated as a trustworthy source of historical weight-loss claims or review evidence.

In September 2018, a federal district court granted summary judgment for the FTC on liability. The case involved unsupported weight-loss claims, a promotional site presented as objective, undisclosed financial relationships, positive-review incentives, gag clauses, and threats or lawsuits aimed at negative reviewers.

A final judgment followed in January 2019. In July 2025, the FTC announced more than $409,000 in payments to 7,481 affected consumers.

**What Roca Labs marketed.**

Roca Labs sold weight-loss products promoted as alternatives to gastric bypass surgery. The FTC challenged safety, effectiveness, rapid weight loss, success-rate, and medical-benefit claims.

Testimonials cannot replace appropriate scientific evidence, especially when the testimonial system is financially influenced.

**The supposedly objective website.**

The FTC alleged that a promotional site was represented as an independent information source even though financial relationships existed behind it.

That tactic is directly relevant to SEO. A seller can create an editorial-looking domain with comparison tables, expert language, and review content. The formatting does not make the page independent.

**Positive review incentives.**

The FTC alleged that customers could receive 50% of the purchase price in exchange for positive “success videos.”

An incentive does not automatically make every statement false, but requiring positive sentiment changes the population of visible evidence and must be disclosed.

**The gag clauses.**

The terms included non-disparagement provisions. The FTC alleged that defendants threatened consumers, demanded money, or used lawsuits to stop negative comments and complaints.

That system pushes praise into public view and criticism out of public view. The resulting reputation profile can look strong precisely because dissent carries a private cost.

**The court and refund record.**

The FTC case page records the 2018 summary judgment, the January 2019 final judgment, and the July 2025 refund distribution.

This is stronger evidence than a pending complaint or anonymous review. The refund announcement refers to defrauded consumers and reports more than $409,000 distributed to 7,481 people.

**What review systems should learn.**

A trustworthy review system should prohibit positive-sentiment requirements, undisclosed discounts, threats over honest opinions, fake independent sites, reviewer identity concealment, and refund conditions that purchase silence.

Publish the policy, keep the moderation log, permit appeals, and separate health claims from testimonials.

**Buyer checklist.**

- Reject non-disparagement clauses covering honest reviews
- Disclose discounts and refunds tied to endorsements
- Do not require positive sentiment
- Verify scientific support for health claims
- Identify who controls editorial-looking sites
- Preserve adverse-event reports
- Publish negative reviews under neutral rules
- Audit agencies and testimonial vendors
- Keep legal threats out of ordinary complaint handling
- Review contracts for Consumer Review Fairness Act risk

## Conclusion

**FAQs.**

**Did a court rule against Roca Labs?**

Yes. The FTC case page records summary judgment on liability and a final judgment.

**Were refunds distributed?**

The FTC announced more than $409,000 in payments to 7,481 consumers in July 2025.

**Are non-disparagement clauses always invalid?**

Context matters, but standardized consumer contract terms restricting honest reviews can violate federal law.

**Can a company offer discounts for reviews?**

It can invite reviews under applicable law and platform policy, but material incentives should be disclosed and positive sentiment should not be required.
