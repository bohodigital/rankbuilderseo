## Preconditions

Google’s first announced Discover-only core update changed the editorial signals publishers should care about: local relevance, less clickbait, deeper original reporting, timeliness, and topic-level expertise. The technical side still matters too, especially large images and clean eligibility.

Google’s February 2026 Discover core update explicitly aimed to improve three things:[@rb-handoff-20-05-source-1]

1. More locally relevant content from websites based in the user’s country.
2. Less sensational content and clickbait.
3. More in-depth, original, timely content from sites with expertise in a topic.

That is an unusually clear editorial brief.

If Discover matters to your site, the 2026 strategy is not:

> Make headlines more outrageous.

It is:

> Build recognizable topic expertise and give Google strong fresh stories with useful media.

**Why Discover is different from classic SEO.**

Google Search starts with a query.

Discover is a personalized feed.

The user did not type:

```text
best way to fix a canonical tag
```

Google predicts that the person may care about a topic.

That means success depends on more than exact keyword targeting.

Discover needs:

- eligible content;
- topic relevance;
- interest match;
- strong media;
- freshness where appropriate;
- clear source identity.

Traffic can be volatile because personalization changes.

**The February 2026 update.**

Google called the release a **Discover core update**.[@rb-handoff-20-05-source-1]

It initially targeted English-language users in the United States and was intended to expand more broadly.

Google also emphasized topic-by-topic expertise.

That matters for multi-topic publications.

A site can demonstrate deep expertise in:

- SEO;
- gardening;
- cooking;
- local news;

without being restricted to one subject across the entire domain.

The section itself needs a coherent body of work.

**Build topic depth, not random volume.**

Suppose a site wants Discover visibility for technical SEO.

Weak section:

```text
1 canonical article
1 celebrity article
1 crypto article
1 HTTP article
1 recipe
```

Strong section:

```text
canonical troubleshooting
redirect migrations
HTTP status diagnostics
Search Console indexing
JavaScript rendering
crawl controls
core update analysis
```

Google’s update specifically says its systems can understand topic-by-topic expertise.[@rb-handoff-20-05-source-1]

This is a reason to build editorial clusters with real depth.

It is not permission to publish 400 shallow pages in one afternoon.

**Originality matters.**

Google explicitly called for more in-depth, original, and timely content.[@rb-handoff-20-05-source-1]

For Discover, originality can be:

- original reporting;
- first-party analysis;
- proprietary data;
- new test;
- real screenshots;
- fresh audit;
- useful opinion from expertise;
- local reporting;
- early explanation of a new change.

A rewrite of twelve other articles can be technically correct and still provide weak information gain.

**Timeliness is not the same as breaking news.**

A timely article answers a current need.

Examples:

- new Google policy;
- software release;
- legal ruling;
- seasonal problem;
- product recall;
- local event;
- new dataset;
- market change.

Evergreen content can appear in Discover, but current interest often creates more opportunity.

Update important evergreen pages when the facts actually change.

Do not update the date every Tuesday and call that freshness.

**Headlines: curiosity without deception.**

Google’s Discover guidance warns against misleading or sensational preview content.[@rb-handoff-20-05-source-2]

Bad:

> GOOGLE JUST DESTROYED SEO FOREVER

Better:

> June 2026 Google Spam Update: What Google Actually Said

The second title still creates curiosity.

It also tells the truth.

A useful Discover headline should make a specific promise the page fulfills.

**Image requirements matter.**

Google recommends compelling, high-quality images and gives unusually specific guidance for Discover.[@rb-handoff-20-05-source-2]

Preferred:

- at least 1,200 pixels wide;
- more than 300,000 total pixels;
- roughly 16:9;
- `max-image-preview:large` enabled;
- relevant to the article;
- specified with suitable metadata.

Avoid:

- site logo as lead image;
- tiny thumbnails;
- generic stock image unrelated to the story;
- giant text baked into the image;
- misleading before/after graphics.

Discover is visually competitive.

Your image is part of the result.

**A practical image specification.**

For RankBuilder:

```text
1600 × 900
original diagram or editorial illustration
minimal text
clear subject
usable on mobile crop
max-image-preview:large
og:image set
```

Do not use an SEO agency logo as the lead art for every company audit.

Create an original evidence diagram instead.

**Local relevance.**

Google’s February update explicitly mentions locally relevant content from websites based in the user’s country.[@rb-handoff-20-05-source-1]

Local relevance can come from:

- local law;
- local prices;
- local weather;
- local events;
- local business data;
- local availability;
- country-specific product support.

Do not manufacture fake location pages.

If the story genuinely differs by country or city, explain the difference.

**Page experience.**

Discover eligibility does not require perfect Core Web Vitals scores.

But page experience still matters.

Avoid:

- interstitial walls;
- broken images;
- autoplay chaos;
- layout shift;
- deceptive ads;
- unreadable mobile layout;
- back-button hijacking.

A feed click is easy to lose because the user was not actively seeking your page.

**Measure Discover separately.**

Search Console has a dedicated Discover performance report for eligible sites.

Track:

- clicks;
- impressions;
- CTR;
- pages;
- countries;
- dates.

Do not compare Discover CTR directly with Web Search CTR as though the surfaces are identical.

For story analysis, calculate:

```text
Discover clicks in first 7 days
Discover impressions
median article age at peak
image type
headline style
topic
```

Then look for repeatable editorial patterns.

**The 2026 Discover content loop.**

**1. Monitor real change.**

Track:

- Google docs;
- regulators;
- product releases;
- data;
- local events;
- industry announcements.

**2. Publish the best explanation.**

Include:

- direct answer;
- what changed;
- who is affected;
- evidence;
- practical action.

**3. Create original lead media.**

Use 16:9 high-resolution visuals.

**4. Connect the topic cluster.**

Link the story to evergreen background pages.

**5. Update when facts change.**

Add visible revision context.

## Failure cases

**FAQ.**

**Does Google Discover require special schema?**

No special Discover schema is required. Standard metadata and content eligibility still matter.

**How wide should Discover images be?**

Google recommends at least 1,200 pixels wide and more than 300,000 total pixels, with large image preview enabled.

**Does clickbait help Discover?**

Google’s 2026 update explicitly says it aims to reduce sensational content and clickbait.

**Can a multi-topic site succeed?**

Yes. Google explicitly says expertise can be understood topic by topic.

**Is Discover traffic stable?**

No. Discover is personalized and can fluctuate substantially.

**Verdict.**

The February 2026 Discover update is a rare case where Google effectively told publishers what editorial direction it wants.

Be original.

Be timely.

Know the topic.

Use strong images.

Stop writing headlines that make the internet worse.
