## Preconditions

Google says there is no secret AI Mode markup, AI text file, or separate ranking system you can optimize with a trick. Pages must still be indexed, snippet-eligible, useful, internally discoverable, and genuinely better than commodity summaries. AI Mode changes the shape of the query, not the need for a strong page.

To improve your odds of being cited in Google AI Mode or AI Overviews in 2026, do the boring things unusually well.

Google’s current guidance says there are **no additional technical requirements** to appear in its generative AI features. A page must be indexed, eligible to appear in Search with a snippet, and compliant with ordinary Search policies.[@rb-handoff-20-01-source-1]

Google also says you do **not** need:

- special AI schema;
- a dedicated AI text file;
- new machine-readable markup invented for AI;
- one page for every possible fan-out query;
- a separate “GEO” version of your website.

What does help is the same foundation Google now emphasizes more aggressively:

- original information;
- clear first-party expertise;
- useful text that machines can parse;
- strong internal links;
- high-quality images and video where appropriate;
- accurate structured data that matches visible content;
- current Merchant Center and Business Profile data for commerce and local intent;
- a page experience that does not make users regret clicking.

**Why this keyword is hot now.**

Google said in May 2026 that AI Mode had surpassed **one billion monthly active users globally** and that AI Mode queries had more than doubled every quarter since launch.[@rb-handoff-20-01-source-2]

Google Trends is also currently promoting a dedicated U.S. report on how people are using AI Mode.

That is a genuine behavior shift, not merely SEO Twitter discovering a new acronym.

People use AI Mode for longer, multi-part, comparative, exploratory questions. Google’s own explanation says AI Mode may use “query fan-out,” issuing multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources to assemble an answer.[@rb-handoff-20-01-source-1][@rb-handoff-20-01-source-3]

That means one user question can trigger a wider retrieval process than one classic keyword.

**Stop thinking only in one-keyword pages.**

A classic page strategy might start with:

```text
best CRM for small business
```

An AI Mode query can look more like:

```text
I run a five-person plumbing company, need call tracking and follow-up automation, hate complicated software, and want something under $200 a month. What should I compare?
```

That query contains multiple hidden tasks:

- identify CRM candidates;
- understand plumbing workflows;
- compare call tracking;
- compare automation;
- filter for price;
- assess ease of use;
- identify small-team fit.

Google’s query fan-out can search across those subtopics.

The correct SEO response is **not** to create thirty thin pages for every sentence fragment.

Google’s new generative AI optimization guide explicitly warns that creating large numbers of pages around possible fan-out query variations primarily to manipulate Search or generative AI responses can violate scaled content abuse policies.[@rb-handoff-20-01-source-4]

Build a page that can answer the real decision.

**What a citation-worthy page looks like.**

A useful AI-search page tends to contain several layers.

**1. A direct answer.**

Do not bury the conclusion under 700 words of throat clearing.

State:

- what is true;
- who it applies to;
- the important exception;
- what the reader should do next.

**2. Verifiable specifics.**

Generic prose is replaceable.

Specifics are retrievable.

Use:

- dates;
- version numbers;
- exact limits;
- test results;
- screenshots;
- official terminology;
- observed examples;
- source links;
- methodology.

**3. Original information.**

Google’s May 2026 optimization guidance repeatedly stresses valuable, unique, non-commodity content.[@rb-handoff-20-01-source-4]

Originality can include:

- a real audit;
- an experiment;
- a comparison framework;
- first-party data;
- a calculator;
- annotated screenshots;
- primary-document analysis;
- a field test;
- a dataset you cleaned yourself.

You do not need to invent journalism for every post.

You do need to contribute something that twenty generated summaries do not already contain.

**The exact technical requirements.**

A page used as a supporting link in AI Mode or AI Overviews must be eligible for ordinary Google Search.[@rb-handoff-20-01-source-1]

Check:

```text
HTTP status: 200
Indexing: allowed
Snippet: allowed
Robots.txt: crawlable
Canonical: coherent
Primary content: available as text
Internal links: present
Mobile experience: usable
```

A page blocked with `noindex` cannot become an AI citation by adding some magical `ai-citation=true` markup because that markup does not exist.

Likewise, `Google-Extended` is not the control for Search inclusion. Google’s documentation distinguishes Search crawling from controls used for some other Google AI systems.[@rb-handoff-20-01-source-1]

**Text still matters.**

Google specifically recommends making important content available in textual form.[@rb-handoff-20-01-source-1]

That does not mean video and images are worthless.

It means the page should not require Google to infer the entire answer from:

- a screenshot;
- a canvas app;
- an embedded video;
- an interactive chart with no labels;
- a PDF image;
- a client-side component that never exposes text.

Use visual media to support the answer, not hide it.

**Images and video matter more, not less.**

Google’s AI optimization guide recommends supporting text with high-quality relevant images and video when appropriate.[@rb-handoff-20-01-source-4]

AI search is increasingly multimodal.

A strong technical article can include:

- architecture diagram;
- before-and-after screenshot;
- code example;
- test output;
- process flow;
- decision tree.

A product comparison can include:

- original product photos;
- sizing visuals;
- interface screenshots;
- comparison tables.

Use descriptive alt text that explains the visual rather than stuffing the primary keyword.

**Entity clarity beats clever branding.**

Make it easy to identify:

- company;
- product;
- person;
- version;
- location;
- date;
- organization relationship.

Weak:

> Their new report changed everything.

Strong:

> Google Search Console introduced a dedicated Generative AI performance report on June 3, 2026.

AI retrieval systems have to connect claims to entities.

Readers do too.

**Internal linking still matters.**

Google’s AI guidance explicitly includes making content easy to find through internal links.[@rb-handoff-20-01-source-1]

Build topic clusters where links reflect actual dependency.

Example:

```text
AI Mode SEO
→ Search Console AI performance report
→ AI Overview measurement
→ canonical URL aggregation
→ Search Console data limits
```

Do not create a giant “related posts” block containing twenty irrelevant links.

Use descriptive anchor text and place links where the relationship is useful.

**Structured data: useful, not magical.**

Google says your structured data should match visible text.[@rb-handoff-20-01-source-1]

There is no special AI Mode schema.

Use existing markup when the page genuinely supports it:

- Article;
- Product;
- Organization;
- LocalBusiness;
- BreadcrumbList;
- VideoObject;
- Recipe;
- Event.

Do not invent `AIOptimized`, `GEOArticle`, or similar fictional types.

**Local and shopping SEO.**

AI Mode increasingly pulls from Google’s structured commerce and local systems.

Google explicitly recommends keeping Merchant Center and Business Profile information current.[@rb-handoff-20-01-source-1]

For local businesses:

- correct categories;
- accurate hours;
- service areas;
- real reviews;
- photos;
- products and services;
- consistent address and phone;
- active owner access.

For ecommerce:

- Merchant Center;
- product identifiers;
- availability;
- price;
- shipping;
- returns;
- high-quality product media.

Your website is not the only source Google uses to understand the business.

**Measure citations separately from traffic.**

Visibility can rise while clicks fall.

Track:

- Search Console AI impressions where available;
- ordinary Search clicks;
- GA4 organic sessions;
- assisted conversions;
- branded query growth;
- page-level conversions;
- citations observed in repeatable test prompts.

Do not report “AI visibility” from a single screenshot.

AI responses can vary by user, session, model, and query wording.

**The practical 30-day plan.**

**Week 1: eligibility.**

Audit indexing, snippets, canonicals, crawlability, internal links, and public content.

**Week 2: information gain.**

Choose the twenty pages with the most commercial or editorial value.

Add:

- real examples;
- sources;
- original diagrams;
- direct answers;
- current dates;
- comparison criteria.

**Week 3: entity and media improvement.**

Clarify products, companies, people, locations, and versions.

Add original images or video where useful.

**Week 4: measurement.**

Baseline:

- AI report impressions;
- organic clicks;
- conversions;
- branded search;
- top landing pages.

Then iterate.

## Failure cases

**FAQ.**

**Do I need GEO instead of SEO?**

No. Google says its normal SEO best practices remain foundational for AI Mode and AI Overviews. “GEO” can be a useful name for measuring generative visibility, but it does not replace technical SEO or content quality.

**Do I need llms.txt to rank in Google AI Mode?**

Google does not require a new AI text file or special machine-readable file for AI Mode inclusion.

**Does schema help AI Mode?**

Accurate structured data can help Google understand supported page information, but there is no special schema required for AI Mode.

**Can I force Google to cite my page?**

No. Eligibility does not guarantee crawling, indexing, ranking, or citation.

**Should I create pages for every fan-out query?**

No. Google explicitly warns against scaled content created around query variations primarily to manipulate rankings or generative AI responses.

**Verdict.**

AI Mode SEO is not a new excuse to manufacture more pages.

It is a stronger reason to make fewer pages more complete, original, technically accessible, and easy to verify.

The sites with the best chance of surviving AI search are not the ones that repeat the keyword most often. They are the ones that give Google, and the reader, something worth citing.
