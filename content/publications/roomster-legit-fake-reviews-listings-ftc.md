## Identified claim

Roomster is a real rental-listing platform. The FTC and six states alleged that it bought tens of thousands of fake reviews, used phony off-platform listings to attract renters, and charged for access to listings advertised as verified and authentic. The FTC’s case page still labels the matter pending.

Roomster is a real rental and roommate-listing platform with a serious enforcement record.

The FTC and six states sued Roomster and its owners in 2022. The complaint alleged tens of thousands of purchased reviews, misleading verification claims, fake or nonexistent listings, and affiliate listings used to drive users toward paid access.

A stipulated order appears in the FTC case timeline, but the agency's page still lists the matter as pending. That procedural status should be preserved.

**The fake-review allegations.**

The government alleged that Roomster bought tens of thousands of four- and five-star reviews from AppWinn. The FTC said the reviews were distributed to appear constant and random.

That detail matters because review fraud is not only about volume. Timing, account age, device use, geography, and language can be manipulated to imitate organic growth.

**The listing allegations.**

Roomster represented that it offered authentic or verified listings. The FTC said its investigation submitted a fictional listing using a U.S. Post Office commercial-facility address and that the platform accepted it.

The complaint also alleged that fake listings on sites such as Craigslist drove users to Roomster, where they paid and then discovered the listings did not exist. Fake positive reviews and fake inventory reinforce each other.

**The AppWinn action.**

The government separately acted against Jonathan Martinez, doing business as AppWinn. The order required cooperation, notification to app stores, identification of fake reviews, a ban on deceptive review sales, and $100,000 for state partners.

A company cannot treat a review supplier as an invisible subcontractor. The buyer remains responsible for what is published on its behalf.

**The 2023 order and procedural status.**

The FTC announcement described a proposed order banning Roomster and its owners from buying or incentivizing reviews, requiring affiliate monitoring, and imposing judgment and penalty terms.

The case page includes a stipulated permanent-injunction document but still labels the case pending. Do not convert every announced term into an assumption about money collected or final closure.

**How renters should verify a listing.**

Search the address independently, compare photos across sites, reverse-image search suspicious photos, verify the owner or property manager, tour in person or by live video, confirm lease authority, and verify the payment recipient.

Never pay by gift card, crypto, wire, or another irreversible method merely because the listing creates urgency.

**How marketplaces should define verification.**

A platform that claims a listing is verified should explain whether it checked identity, address, ownership, lease authority, phone, duplicate images, payment information, or merely email access.

A badge without a published definition is interface decoration, the online equivalent of a man in a vest standing beside an unlocked door.

**Buyer checklist.**

- Verify the legal property owner
- Confirm the physical address
- Tour by live video or in person
- Reverse-image search listing photos
- Confirm lease authority
- Verify the payment recipient
- Read subscription and renewal terms
- Save listing and messages
- Avoid irreversible payments
- Report fake listings with preserved evidence

## Conclusion

**FAQs.**

**Is Roomster banned from operating?**

The reviewed FTC materials describe review-related restrictions and monetary terms, not a universal platform ban.

**Was every Roomster listing fake?**

No. The government alleged significant fake-listing and verification problems, not that every listing was false.

**Are judgment amounts the same as money paid?**

No. Judgment, suspended amount, penalties, and collected funds can differ.

**Is AppWinn the same company as Roomster?**

No. AppWinn was the separate review supplier named in the related action.

**Can a legitimate listing still appear on Roomster?**

Possibly. Verify each listing independently rather than infer authenticity from the platform name.

**Final verdict.**

Roomster is a real platform with a substantial fake-review and listing-authenticity enforcement record. Pay only after confirming the property, owner, availability, subscription terms, and payment recipient outside the platform’s own trust signals.
